# Original proposal: Reducing regimen confusion in aged care medication administration

*Author: Bonnie Lin (July–August 2026). Plain-text copy of `original-proposal.docx`, kept as the source brief. The product has since moved to home use; see `../DESIGN_RATIONALE.md` §1.*

Problem Definition & Context
Nearly half of adults aged 65+ are affected by polypharmacy (which is defined as taking 5 or more medications regularly), with complex regimens involving varied dosing times, differing frequencies, and conflicting food requirements, creating a major source of confusion. This confusion-driven mismanagement is a distinct and significant cause of medication non-adherence, which is different to simply forgetting to take a dose. In residential aged care facilities, regiment complexity burdens both cognitively impaired residents as well as the nursing staff who manually sort, dispense and chart doses for dozens of individuals per shift. Inappropriate medication use contributes significantly to preventable hospital admissions among older adults, while manual charting remains a time-consuming and error-prone process that strains already stretched facility teams. A solution is therefore needed that simplifies regiment complexity at the point of care and eases administration workload without disrupting existing workflows. 
Goals & Strategic Approach
This proposal aims to reduce medication confusion for aged care residents on complex regimens and reduce the administration/charting burden on nursing staff. Key objectives: reduce a resident’s daily regimen to a single decision point per dose, confirm intake within seconds of consumption, and integrate dose records directly into the facility’s existing electronic medication administration record (eMAR) system. The strategy combines automated dose pre-sorting with sensor-based confirmation and direct clinical software integration, rather than a standalone consumer app. 
Innovation & System Integration
The proposed solution is a smart delivery cup. Nursing staff or a pharmacist loads a rotating pill carousel weekly, pre-sorted by each medication’s dosing rule. At each scheduled time, the cup prompts the resident; a release gate drops only that dose’s tablets into the spout, carried down with water and swallowed as normal, without crushing or dissolving, preserving coated and extended-release formulations. A sip-triggered sensor confirms intake and logs it directly to the facility’s existing eMAR system, replacing a manual charting step, rather than a separate caregiver app as used by home dispensers, such as MedMinder. This targets facility-scale workflow integration, a gap unaddressed by current consumer focused dispensers. Additionally, this cup also has a friendly, non-institutional design, which will reduce the stigma some residents associate with clinical aids.

Impact & Expected Outcomes
The pilot is expected to cut nursing staff time on manual dose charting by 40-50%, reduce confusion-related missed or incorrect doses among residents on 5+ medications by an estimated 20%, and improve dose-confirmation accuracy in facility records. Residents gain greater independence in dosing, while nursing staff and prescribers gain accurate, real-time adherence data within their existing clinical workflow.
